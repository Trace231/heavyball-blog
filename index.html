Abstract

What happens when an optimization method admits neither a familiar Lyapunov certificate nor a periodic counterexample? These notes study a concrete Heavy-Ball parameter regime where the prescribed two-point linear/quadratic certificate fails, yet every admissible fixed objective still exhibits global linear convergence.

Exact separation witness

At the normalized parameters below, the prescribed certificate family fails and no nonconstant finite cycle exists in any finite dimension.

(q*, a*, b*) = (1/100, 113/50, 3/5)

Uniform convergence box

An explicit parameter box around the witness has a uniform global linear rate for every finite dimension, objective, initialization, and time.

Longer-range geometry

Two consecutive observations can support a locally expanding step. Interpolation constraints linking observations farther apart prevent that expansion from persisting.

1. The question behind the gap

With a fixed step size and momentum, Heavy-Ball updates follow

xt+1 = xt − a∇f(xt) + b(xt − xt−1).

For a whole class of smooth strongly convex objectives, one usually seeks either a decreasing energy that certifies convergence or a cycle that refutes it. The second Heavy-Ball riddle asks whether there can be parameters admitting neither the specified Lyapunov certificate nor a finite periodic counterexample.

2. Main results

Theorem A — An exact separation witness. The prescribed two-point linear/quadratic Lyapunov family fails at the witness above, while every nonconstant finite cycle is excluded over the full admissible function class.
Theorem B — Uniform global linear convergence. Throughout an explicit box of radius 10−5 around the witness, every admissible trajectory converges globally at a uniform linear rate. The constants are deliberately conservative and are not an acceleration claim as the condition number varies.

The notes prove the certificate obstruction, exclude finite periods through a single polynomial positivity problem, and then extend the same structure to infinite time without assuming convergence along the way.

3. Why two points are not enough

The central obstruction is informational. A common energy that sees only two consecutive positions, gradients, and objective gaps cannot distinguish objectives that share the same current record but have different future behavior. By scaling a single interpolated construction, the notes join identical records across objectives and force an impossible chain of inequalities.

This does not mean that each fixed objective lacks a Lyapunov function. An objective-dependent energy can use the whole future trajectory; what fails is one finite-valued formula that depends only on the displayed two-point record and works for every objective.

4. What remains open

The current result classifies one robust convergent region rather than the entire Heavy-Ball parameter domain. The notes leave open the global classification, the relationship between failure of universal convergence and finite cycles, sharper worst-case rates, and how much additional history an effective common certificate needs.
